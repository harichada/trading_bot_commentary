# US-Equity Return Effects That Survive Realistic Costs at Short Horizons — 2026 Literature Review

**Scope:** Intraday-to-few-week horizons, US equities, from the perspective of a retail-scale systematic trader using 5-minute bars and a standard retail broker (no direct market access, no co-location). Compiled September 25, 2026. Figures are as reported in cited sources; where no cost-adjusted number exists in the literature, that is stated rather than estimated.

## 1. Overnight vs. intraday return split

**Lou, Polk & Skouras, "A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns,"** *JFE* 134(1), 2019, pp. 192–213. [LSE copy](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf) · [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19300650)

Across 14 well-known cross-sectional strategies, profits are earned almost entirely in *either* the overnight *or* the intraday window, with an offsetting reversal in the other — a decomposition, not a new anomaly. The overnight/intraday spread varies up to ~2%/month with rebalancing pressure. Sample: CRSP data, ~1993–2013. Implementable cheaply (one decision on 2 trades/day: hold overnight or flatten) with 5-minute bars, but it routes existing signals rather than creating new alpha.

**Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan, "The Overnight Drift,"** *RFS* 36(9), 2023; NY Fed SR917. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3546173)

US index futures earned outsized returns in the 2:00–3:00 a.m. ET window (European open): ~3.6% annualized in that hour, with the full overnight session ~2.6% annualized — over half of the 4.3% annualized close-to-close return, 1998–2019. **Decay explicitly documented**: the authors' own 2026 follow-up, **"The Disappearing Overnight Drift"** ([Liberty Street Economics, July 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)), reports the effect has **averaged close to zero since 2021**. One of the cleanest cases of a real-time effect decaying to nothing within years of publication. Verdict: historically real, now dead.

## 2. Intraday momentum and the last-half-hour effect

**Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum,"** *JFE* 129(2), 2018. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)

SPY's first-half-hour return predicts its last-half-hour return, in- and out-of-sample (1993–2013). Predictive R² 1.6% (first half hour) to 2.6% (adding hour 12); out-of-sample R² 1.4–2.0%. Stronger on high-volatility/volume/news days and across 10 other liquid ETFs. No cost-adjusted P&L reported — this is an R² predictability result, not a backtest. **Decay explicitly documented**: a follow-up, **"Understanding Intraday Momentum Strategies"** ([CoLab](https://colab.ws/articles/10.1002/fut.22375)), extends the sample ~7 years post-2013 and finds the predictability **disappears out-of-sample**; a Markov-switching model suggests the effect is regime-dependent, a much weaker and unpublished-cost claim. Verdict: real 1993–2013, decayed since.

## 3. Opening range breakout (ORB)

**Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?"** SSRN 4416622 (2023), QQQ/TQQQ 5-min ORB, Jan 2016–Feb 2023. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622) Headline (gross): TQQQ variant 1,484% cumulative vs. 169% buy-and-hold QQQ.

**Independent replication is decisive**: [giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq) shows the $138,639 profit figure assumes **zero slippage**; net P&L **crosses zero at ~2.2¢/share** slippage against a ~1¢ QQQ spread — the entire edge lives inside the spread. With $0.02 entry + $0.04 stop slippage, profit on 1,775 trades collapses from $138,639 to **$4,860 (–96%)**. An NQ-futures confirmation filter reaches t=2.05 (~$0.125/share edge), but **76% of that filtered edge came from 2022 alone**, with losing years in 2017/2020/2023 — regime-dependent, not structural. Sharpe (0.77) barely beats buy-and-hold (0.72).

**Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market,"** SSRN 4729284 (2024) — 5-min ORB restricted to "stocks in play" (elevated relative volume/news) across >7,000 stocks, 2016–2023. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284) Reported net of stated costs: ~1,600% cumulative, Sharpe 2.4–2.81, beta ≈ 0; the effect is much weaker on the unfiltered universe. No independent replication was found; given the QQQ paper's known cost-fragility, and the harder execution reality of thousands of volatile small caps via a retail broker (partial fills, locate constraints), this should be treated with comparable skepticism until replicated.

**Zarattini & Aziz, VWAP paper,** SSRN 4631351 — argues VWAP-referenced entries cut effective slippage vs. naive market orders; a cost-mitigation overlay, not an independent edge.

## 4. Short-term reversal (daily/intraday) and cost sensitivity

**Jegadeesh (1990),** *JF* 45(3) — 1-month reversal: decile spread 2.49%/month, 1934–1987, gross. de Groot, Huij & Zhou, **"Another Look at Trading Costs and Short-Term Reversal Profits"** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID1894614_code340546.pdf?abstractid=1605049&mirid=1)), find reversal profits concentrated in small/illiquid names and that transaction costs consume most of the gross alpha (round-trip cost estimates of 34–75bp against reversal signals of similar magnitude); large-cap restriction cuts both return and turnover. Weekly-reversal variants report **~30–50bp/week net**, but only with careful universe restriction and buy/hold-band execution. **Novy-Marx & Velikov, "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs,"** *RFS* 29(1), 2016 ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)): anomalies with >50% monthly turnover (reversal is the highest-turnover anomaly in their taxonomy) rarely survive costs without an asymmetric buy/hold threshold. Implementable on 5-min bars (signal needs only daily closes), but requires trading hundreds of small/illiquid names — exactly where retail execution (no DMA, PFOF routing) is worst. Net edge for a simple retail bot is likely at or below zero without sophisticated execution.

## 5. Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) and its decline

Bernard & Thomas (1990) established SUE-decile drift; modern review: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214635020303750); [Caltech WP](https://jkatz.caltech.edu/documents/28622/peads.pdf). Original SUE decile hedge portfolio: ~8–9%/quarter (~35% annualized gross); analyst-forecast-based SUE spreads are smaller (~3%/quarter). **Decline explicitly documented**: high-minus-low SUE spread fell from ~5%/quarter in the 1980s–90s to ≤3% by the late 2010s; effect reported insignificant for non-microcaps by ~2001 and ~zero for large caps by ~2006. Transaction costs are estimated to consume **70–100% of gross profits** from earnings-momentum strategies. Cause is debated: falling costs enabling more arbitrage vs. declining signal informativeness (some authors find the "decay" is not significant once informativeness is controlled for). Easily implementable on 5-min bars/retail broker (holding period is days-weeks); binding constraint is thin edge vs. cost, not execution. Verdict: still real in small/low-coverage names, gone in large caps.

**Pre-earnings drift:** Easton, Gao & Gao, SSRN 1786697 — firms announcing after industry peers show predictable pre-announcement drift from peers' surprises; long-short >100bp/month. No explicit cost-adjusted figure found, but monthly rebalancing implies modest costs relative to signal.

## 6. News- and sentiment-based return prediction

**Tetlock (2007),** *JF* 62(3) — high WSJ media pessimism predicts downward price pressure followed by reversion within days, and predicts high subsequent volume; effects are a few bp/day, mean-reverting within about a week. Foundational evidence sentiment is a noise/liquidity-trader proxy, not new fundamental information; not itself a single-stock trading rule.

**Ke, Kelly & Xiu, "Predicting Returns with Text Data,"** NBER WP 26186 (2019) — a supervised sentiment-extraction method (screen terms → topic weights → penalized-likelihood score) applied to Dow Jones Newswires beats dictionary-based sentiment at near-term return prediction. No explicit net-of-cost, standalone-strategy Sharpe found; built on a low-latency proprietary newswire feed a retail trader is unlikely to match — most such signal value decays within minutes to hours.

**Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?"** SSRN 4412788 / [arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619), forthcoming *JFE* — GPT-based good/bad/irrelevant headline classification correlates with subsequent daily returns and beats dictionary sentiment; smaller/older models (GPT-1/2, BERT) fail to replicate it, suggesting return predictability is emergent in large models. This search found no peer-reviewed, cost-adjusted, genuinely out-of-sample (post-2023) replication. Given daily rebalancing needs, post-2023 proliferation of commercial LLM sentiment products (likely compressing the edge, per §11), and concentration of raw predictability in small/wide-spread names, retail economics are materially worse than the frictionless backtest.

**2024–2026 LLM/ML intraday prediction, cost-adjusted:** [llm-stock-predictor-postmortem](https://github.com/AbhayPhalswal/llm-stock-predictor-postmortem) built and live-paper-traded an LLM day-trading system (NSE), then rebuilt with 10 years of data/21 sources and found **no edge after costs**: at 20bp round-trip, daily round-tripping costs ~50%/year in fees alone. Not US equities, but the cost arithmetic generalizes directly. Across the broader 2024–2026 LLM/transformer stock-prediction literature surveyed, most papers report accuracy/R² or gross backtested Sharpe without realistic retail-cost-adjusted, truly out-of-sample live results; where such results exist, they are typically negative (as above).

## 7. Time-series/cross-sectional momentum (monthly) and 1-month reversal

**Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, "Time Series Momentum,"** *JFE* 104(2), 2012 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2089463)) — past 1–12 month returns predict continuation across 58 futures instruments, partially reversing over 12–36 months. Vol-scaled TSMOM alpha 1.27%/month; unscaled 0.41%; comparable cross-sectional momentum (XSMOM) alpha 0.95%/month. This is diversified futures, not single-name equities; the closer single-stock analogue is Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) cross-sectional momentum (3–12 month formation), which Fama & French call the "premier anomaly," but it is a longer-horizon effect than this report's core scope — mainly useful as a trend/regime filter, and industry/sector momentum (§9) is often stronger than single-stock momentum. 1-month reversal is covered in §4 and shares its cost fragility.

## 8. Volatility-managed portfolios

**Moreira & Muir, "Volatility-Managed Portfolios,"** *JF* 72(4), 2017 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2659431)) — scaling factor exposure inversely with recent realized volatility raises Sharpe ratios and utility because vol changes aren't offset by proportional expected-return changes, across market, value, momentum, profitability, investment, BAB, and carry. **Critique explicitly documented**: Cederburg et al. (2020) show gains are not achievable out-of-sample/real-time due to estimation error; Barroso & Detzel (2021) show transaction costs from time-varying leverage erode most gains, with only market and momentum partially robust ([disappearing-profitability thread](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418123000551)). Monthly rebalancing is trivial to execute, but net-of-cost real-time edge is weak-to-absent for most factors. Verdict: popular but largely fails to survive costs/real-time implementation except narrowly (market, momentum).

## 9. Retail order flow and attention effects

**Barber & Odean, "All That Glitters,"** *RFS* 21(2), 2008 ([Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Attention/All%20that%20Glitters.pdf)) — individuals are net buyers of extreme-return/extreme-volume/news stocks (buy-sell imbalance 16.17% for in-news vs. −1.84% for no-news, an 18-point spread), but this attention-driven buying **does not generate superior returns**: bought stocks subsequently underperform sold stocks, and the heaviest attention-chasers underperform.

**"Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users"** ([IIMB PDF](https://www.iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf)) — post-2020 confirmation via the Boehmer-Jones-Zhang-Zhang (BJZZ) retail-trade-identification algorithm. Stocks most heavily bought by Robinhood users underperform by **−4.7% over the following 20 days**; long-short strategies on extreme retail order-imbalance quintiles earn **−14.8% annualized** among heavily-retail-traded names vs. **+6.6%** among others — retail order flow is a fade, not a follow, signal. A retail bot can't easily observe real-time aggregate retail imbalance, but the practical implication — avoid/fade unusual-volume, headline-driven, high-attention names rather than chase them — is directly actionable, though it is effectively a version of short-term reversal (§4) concentrated in the hardest-to-execute names.

## 10. Intraday seasonality of returns/volume; gap-fill statistics

**U-shape:** volume, volatility, and spreads are highest near the open/close and lowest at midday — a widely replicated stylized fact ([survey](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3899820); [NASDAQ study](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4792199)). This is primarily a cost/microstructure fact, not a directional signal: spreads are widest exactly when momentum/breakout signals (e.g., ORB) are strongest, which is part of why the QQQ ORB result above is so slippage-sensitive.

**Gap-fill:** aggregate gap-closure ~76% across all gap days, but collapses for large gaps (>5%, ~9.6% fill) and especially for large gaps in high-volatility, outside-prior-range conditions (~21% fill); gap-downs fill somewhat more than gap-ups (62.2% vs. 58.8%). Small no-catalyst gaps fill within 5 sessions ~70–75% of the time; earnings-driven gaps fill within a month <30% of the time ([summary](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/gap-fill-trading-strategies/); [NQ study](https://tradingstats.net/gap-fill-strategy/)). These are practitioner/backtest sources, not peer-reviewed journals, and "fill %" is not an expectancy calculation — the large, news-driven gaps least likely to fill are also the ones a naive fade strategy would be most tempted to trade. No clean net-of-cost annualized return for a pure gap-fade rule was found.

## 11. Post-publication decay and realistic cost benchmarks (context for all of the above)

**McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?"** *JF* 71(1), 2016 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2156623)) — across 97 published predictors, out-of-sample returns are ~26% lower than in-sample, and **post-publication returns ~58% lower than in-sample** (roughly another 32 points attributable to publication-informed trading). Decay is largest for the highest in-sample-return, highest-idiosyncratic-risk, lowest-liquidity predictors — exactly the profile of ORB, PEAD, reversal, and intraday momentum above. Default prior: discount any published Sharpe ratio by roughly a third to a half before assuming it is still live.

**Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016)** vs. **Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz, "Trading Costs"** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3229719), $1.7T of real institutional execution data, 21 markets, 19 years) bracket realistic costs: naive high-turnover strategies are usually cost-prohibitive without explicit mitigation, but patient institutional execution costs are an order of magnitude below academic price-impact assumptions. Retail sits structurally closer to the pessimistic end — no execution desk, no patience over days, and PFOF routing rather than direct NBBO access.

## Ranked table: credible net-of-cost edge for a retail bot in 2026

| Rank | Effect | Horizon | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PEAD in small/mid-cap, low-coverage names | Days–weeks | Yes, modest — thin but real gross edge, cost is the main risk |
| 2 | "Stocks in play" 5-min ORB (news/volume-filtered) | Intraday | Plausible but unverified — no independent replication found; execution risk on volatile small caps is real |
| 3 | Fading retail-attention/meme-stock froth | 1–4 weeks | Yes, directionally — well-replicated underperformance; best used as an avoid/fade filter |
| 4 | Overnight/intraday routing of existing signals | Daily | Yes, as a cheap filter, not a standalone alpha source |
| 5 | Large-cap short-term reversal with buy/hold-band execution | Days | Marginal — net edge exists institutionally but needs sophistication most retail bots lack |

## Popular effects that do NOT survive costs / are not credible in 2026

- **QQQ/TQQQ unfiltered 5-min ORB** — break-even at ~2.2¢/share slippage vs. a ~1¢ spread; replication shows the headline return is a zero-slippage artifact, and even the significant filtered version is 76% concentrated in one year.
- **2:00–3:00 a.m. overnight drift** — real 1998–2021, ~zero since 2021 per the authors' own follow-up.
- **Naive SPY intraday momentum (first half hour → last half hour)** — documented 1993–2013, disappears out-of-sample afterward.
- **Volatility-managed portfolios** across most factors — fail real-time implementation and transaction costs except for market/momentum.
- **PEAD in large caps** — essentially zero since the mid-2000s; costs consume 70–100% of gross profit even where signal remains.
- **LLM/news-sentiment daily long-short strategies** — strong gross backtests, no cost-adjusted true-out-of-sample replication found; an analogous rigorously-tested LLM day-trading system found no edge after realistic costs.
- **"Fade the gap" as a standalone rule** — the fill-rate statistic is real but not an expectancy calculation; the least-likely-to-fill gaps are the most news-driven ones a naive rule would target.

## Bottom line

No effect surveyed offers a large, uncrowded, easily implementable edge with 5-minute bars and a no-DMA retail broker in 2026. The most defensible path is using modest, well-documented effects (PEAD in under-covered names, attention-driven fade, overnight/intraday routing) as portfolio-construction and timing overlays rather than standalone signals, while discounting every backtested Sharpe ratio in this literature by McLean-Pontiff's ~30–50% publication-decay factor and further discounting for retail (non-DMA, PFOF, no patient execution) costs before sizing live capital.
