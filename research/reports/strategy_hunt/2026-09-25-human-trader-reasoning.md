# How Skilled Discretionary Equity Traders Reason: A Cognitive Architecture for a Trading Bot

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Scope:** Synthesis of published playbooks, trader interviews, prop-firm curricula, and academic research on expert trader decision-making, translated into a software-mappable cognitive architecture.

## Why this matters for the bot

Discretionary traders who survive years in the market are not running a single predictive model. They run a *pipeline*: context first, candidates second, a small library of confirmable setups third, regime-adjusted aggression fourth, rule-based trade management fifth, hard risk limits sixth, and a feedback loop that prunes what stops working. Academic work on expert decision-making backs this up — Gary Klein's naturalistic decision-making research found that experts in time-pressured domains use **recognition-primed decision-making**: they don't compare many options, they pattern-match to one plausible course of action and mentally simulate it before committing ([Wikipedia: Naturalistic decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalistic_decision-making)). Lo and Repin's psychophysiology studies of professional FX and derivatives traders found that emotional/autonomic responses are present in *all* traders, including the most experienced, and that emotion is not noise to be eliminated but a channel that supports intuitive judgment — except when reactivity is too intense, which predicts *worse* performance ([NBER Digest on Lo & Repin](https://www.nber.org/digest/mar02/measuring-stress-financial-traders)). This is the throughline of the whole report: pros do not suppress pattern recognition or emotion, they **structure and bound** it with checklists, if/then rules, and hard stops. That structure is exactly what a bot can encode.

Below, each of the eight requested elements is grounded in named sources, then translated into (a) the data/signals a bot needs and (b) a concrete decision rule.

## 1. Pre-market preparation

Mike Bellafiore's SMB Capital playbook treats pre-market prep as the foundation of the day: traders build a short list of "stocks in play," define catalysts, and write an if/then plan before the bell rather than reacting live ([SMB Training: 40 lessons from The PlayBook](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/40-trading-lessons-from-the-playbook); [TraderLion PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/)). Retail/prop day-trading guides converge on a similar routine: scan gappers, confirm each gap has a real catalyst within about a minute of looking (earnings beat + raised guidance, FDA approval, M&A, contract win, analyst action), mark premarket high/low, prior-day high/low/close, and VWAP, then write entry triggers, stops, targets, and size for each name *before* the open — literally checked off a list so steps aren't skipped under time pressure ([The Trader's Insight: pre-market routine](https://www.thetradersinsight.com/blog/how-to-build-a-pre-market-trading-routine); [Bulls on Wall Street: daily scan routine](https://www.bullsonwallstreet.com/post/scan-explosive-stocks-day-trade); [DayTradingToolkit: pre-market gappers guide](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/beginners-guide/pre-market-gappers-evaluation-guide)). Linda Raschke's process is explicitly top-down: assess the higher timeframe first (she uses at least a monthly view), classify the likely day type (trend vs. mean-reversion), *then* drill into individual names and pre-place resting orders at the levels that plan implies ([OptionsTradingIQ: Raschke rules](https://optionstradingiq.com/linda-raschke-rules-for-trading/)).

**Signals a bot needs:** overnight index futures/ETF direction and gap size; economic calendar events and their scheduled time; sector ETF relative gaps; VIX level and change; a ranked gapper list (symbol, gap %, catalyst text, premarket $ volume, premarket relative volume); prior day's high/low/close and VWAP per candidate; market-wide breadth snapshot (advancers/decliners, new highs/lows).

**Decision rule:** Before the session opens, the bot builds a `daily_plan` object: (1) classify macro backdrop (risk-on/risk-off/neutral) from futures gap + VIX delta + breadth; (2) generate a watchlist of the top N (e.g., 5–10) symbols ranked by a composite "in-play score" (Section 2); (3) for each watchlist symbol, pre-compute entry trigger price, invalidation price, and initial size *before* the open; (4) set the day's starting risk budget (Section 6) conditioned on the macro classification — e.g., reduce max daily risk by 50% if VIX gapped up >10% or no clear catalysts exist on the watchlist. If a symbol lacks an identifiable catalyst string, it is excluded from trade-eligible status regardless of chart pattern, mirroring the "no catalyst within 60 seconds → red flag" rule traders use.

## 2. Stock selection — what makes a name "in play"

SMB's definition of a stock in play is explicit: "any stock that has a catalyst (news, big technical level, large percentage move, unusually high volume)" and traders should narrow to their best 2–3 names rather than watch everything ([SMB Training: PlayBook Checkup](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/the-playbook-checkup-change-in-fundamentals); [SMB: 40 lessons](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/40-trading-lessons-from-the-playbook)). Gapper-evaluation guides operationalize this into filterable variables: gap size buckets (1–4% weak, 5–15% most tradeable, 15–40% strong, 40%+ high-risk), premarket relative volume of 3–5x+ normal pace, float bucket (under ~10–20M shares = highest volatility/hardest to control; 20–100M = more tradeable for size; 100M+ = orderly but needs a bigger catalyst to move), and whether price is holding near premarket highs (bullish continuation) versus fading (distribution) ([DayTradingToolkit gappers guide](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/beginners-guide/pre-market-gappers-evaluation-guide)). Minervini's swing-selection layer adds relative strength and stage analysis: candidates must be in Stage 2 uptrend, above rising 50/150/200-day moving averages in the correct order, within a defined distance of 52-week highs, and showing high relative strength versus the market before a specific entry pattern (e.g., volatility contraction) is even considered ([FinerMarketPoints: Minervini SEPA/VCP guide](https://www.finermarketpoints.com/post/what-is-mark-minervini-s-trading-strategy-the-complete-sepa-vcp-guide); [ChartMill: Minervini strategy](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/fundamental-analysis-investing-strategies/464-Mark-Minervini-Strategy-Think-and-Trade-Like-a-Champion-Part-1)). Qullamaggie's swing/episodic-pivot selection adds a "quiet base" filter — the best episodic pivots occur in stocks that were flat for 3–6 months before the catalyst, and names that already had a recent EP show a higher failure rate on the next one ([Qullamaggie: How to master Episodic Pivots](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)).

**Signals a bot needs:** gap % and $ magnitude; catalyst classification (earnings/guidance, FDA, M&A, contract, upgrade/downgrade, macro, unknown); premarket and intraday relative volume (vs. 20/30-day average at same time-of-day); float and short interest; distance from 52-week high; relative strength rank vs. sector/index over 1/3/6/12-month windows; realized range expansion (today's range or ATR vs. 20-day ATR); days since last comparable catalyst-driven move in the same name.

**Decision rule:** Compute an `in_play_score` as a weighted sum: catalyst_present (binary gate — 0 disqualifies), relative_volume (log-scaled), gap_or_range_expansion (z-score vs. 20-day), relative_strength_percentile (vs. universe), and a base-quality bonus (flat/quiet 3–6 months prior for EP-style candidates, or trend-template pass for swing candidates). Rank the eligible universe daily/intraday; cap the active watchlist at a fixed N (e.g., 3 intraday, 8–10 swing) so the bot — like SMB traders — is forced to focus size and attention rather than diffusing it. Penalize or exclude names with a comparable catalyst move within the lookback window (recency penalty), mirroring the EP "already moved recently → lower quality" rule.

## 3. Setup recognition — a small playbook, traded on confirmation

Every source converges on the same structural point: professionals trade a *short, memorized list* of repeatable setups, not ad hoc predictions, and they enter on price confirming the setup rather than anticipating it. Bellafiore: "be patient with entry and make sure the stock entered meets the criteria for one of your better setups… your job as a trader is not to be right or wrong but to manage risk" ([TraderLion PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/)). Raschke's five canonical patterns (Anti, Holy Grail, Turtle Soup, 80/20, Momentum Pinball) are described as "repeatable mechanics that work because they're executed the same way, session after session" with explicit checklists for market state, bias, setup grade, and risk per trade so "entries are confirmations, not debates" ([TradingLiteracy: Raschke's 5 patterns](https://tradingliteracy.com/linda-raschke-5-trading-patterns/); [NewTraderU: Raschke's 12 rules](https://www.newtraderu.com/2015/05/28/linda-raschkes-12-technical-trading-rules/)). Qullamaggie's episodic pivot has a precise, mechanical trigger: wait for the first 1-minute candle to complete, enter on a break of the opening-range high (or add on 5-minute/60-minute highs for late entries), with the stop always at the low of the day and never wider than 1–1.5x ADR ([Qullamaggie: Episodic Pivots](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)). Minervini's VCP entry is a "pivot" buy point off a tightening volatility-contraction pattern within the Trend Template context, with a hard 7–8% maximum loss defined *before* entry ([StockManiacs: Minervini SEPA](https://www.stockmaniacs.net/mark-minervini-sepa-strategy/)). Adam Grimes frames this as deliberately "keeping the universe small" — a restricted toolkit of setups with a defined statistical edge and a structural invalidation point (e.g., his inside-day concept, where the whole prior range becomes the invalidation boundary), which avoids the pattern-recognition failure mode of "seeing faces in clouds" ([Better System Trader: Adam Grimes interview](https://bettersystemtrader.com/055-adam-grimes-market-behavior/)).

**Signals a bot needs:** for each registered playbook setup, a machine-checkable definition: precondition filters (trend/stage, base duration, RS rank), a trigger price/event (opening-range-high break, pivot breakout above a defined level, pullback to a moving average with reclaim, etc.), a confirmation requirement (volume threshold, time-elapsed-since-trigger, close-above vs. intrabar touch), and an explicit invalidation price computed at signal time.

**Decision rule:** Maintain a `playbook` registry (setup_id, precondition function, trigger function, invalidation function, historical expectancy). A candidate becomes trade-eligible only when preconditions pass AND the trigger event actually occurs (never on a predicted/anticipated level) AND a confirmation filter is satisfied (e.g., volume ≥ X% of the pace needed to hit average daily volume, or price closes rather than just wicks through the level). At trigger time, compute and store the invalidation price atomically with the entry — the same instant, not after — so stop placement is never discretionary after the fact. Setups not in the registry are not tradeable, full stop; this operationalizes "trade your setups, not your opinions."

## 4. Regime and market context — when to press, when to sit out

Raschke's approach begins with classifying the "day type" before trading individual names: trend days call for holding through pullbacks and exiting near the close (avoiding overnight risk while capturing the whole move); range/mean-reversion days call for fading extremes ([OptionsTradingIQ: Raschke rules](https://optionstradingiq.com/linda-raschke-rules-for-trading/)). She explicitly flags that "a trader caught off guard will often experience his largest losses on a trend day" by trying to fade strength/weakness prematurely — i.e., applying a range-day playbook in trend conditions is a primary loss driver ([OptionsTradingIQ: Raschke rules](https://optionstradingiq.com/linda-raschke-rules-for-trading/)). Adam Grimes' market-structure framework treats trend, range, and transition as the three fundamental states a trader must classify before selecting which setups are even in scope, and argues discretionary skill largely consists of correctly reading which state the market is in ([Better System Trader: Adam Grimes interview](https://bettersystemtrader.com/055-adam-grimes-market-behavior/); [Art & Science of Technical Analysis, Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Art-Science-Technical-Analysis-Strategies/dp/1118115120)). Tape-reading practice adds a faster-timescale regime signal: acceleration in prints-per-second and the presence of large block prints are used as live evidence of a transition from range to trend, with aggressive lifted offers vs. hit bids read as directional conviction ([Bookmap: reading trade size/speed/aggression](https://bookmap.com/blog/whats-inside-the-tape-how-to-read-trade-size-speed-and-aggression); [JAW Trades: reading time & sales](https://www.jawtrades.com/learn/how-to-read-the-tape)). Prop desks formalize the "sit out" option directly into risk limits — daily loss limits (commonly 4–5% of account) and drawdown caps that force flat/no-new-risk once breached ([TradeZella: prop firm challenge risk framework](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/pass-prop-firm-challenge); [ThinkCapital: drawdown rules](https://www.thinkcapital.com/prop-firm-drawdown-rules/); [NinjaTrader Prop: risk parameters](https://prop.ninjatrader.com/blogs/prop-firm-risk-parameters/)).

**Signals a bot needs:** intraday classification features — realized range vs. average true range so far, number of VWAP crosses (fewer crosses = trend day tell), breadth (advance/decline, % of stocks above key MAs), VIX level and intraday change, index correlation of the watchlist names, tape acceleration (prints/sec, ratio of aggressive-buy to aggressive-sell prints, block-print frequency).

**Decision rule:** Classify each session (and re-classify periodically intraday) into `trend`, `range`, or `transition/uncertain` using a rules-of-thumb model (e.g., few VWAP crosses + one-directional breadth + rising realized range → trend; frequent VWAP crosses + flat breadth → range). Gate which setups from the Section 3 registry are active based on regime (trend-following setups enabled only in `trend`/`transition`; mean-reversion setups enabled only in `range`). Scale the position-size multiplier and the number of concurrent positions up in confirmed trend regimes with supportive breadth, and down (or to zero — flat, cash, no new entries) when VIX spikes sharply intraday, breadth is conflicting, or the regime classifier itself is low-confidence — directly mirroring the prop-desk practice of cutting size or standing aside rather than forcing trades in unclear tape.

## 5. Trade management — partials, stops, letting winners run, news mid-trade

The management layer is where nearly every named source has an explicit, mechanical rule set. Minervini: define maximum risk (7–8% stop) before entry, move the stop to breakeven as the trade works, then trail it to protect gains; exits also trigger on a profit target or a close below a moving-average trend signal (e.g., 50-day EMA) ([FinerMarketPoints: SEPA/VCP guide](https://www.finermarketpoints.com/post/what-is-mark-minervini-s-trading-strategy-the-complete-sepa-vcp-guide); [ChartMill: Minervini strategy](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/fundamental-analysis-investing-strategies/464-Mark-Minervini-Strategy-Think-and-Trade-Like-a-Champion-Part-1)). Qullamaggie sells roughly a third to half of the position after 3–5 days or at 2–3R, moves the remaining stop to breakeven, and trails the runner against the 10- or 20-day moving average rather than a fixed price ([search synthesis of Qullamaggie method, cross-checked against Episodic Pivots primary page: stop at day's low, size added on 1-min/5-min highs](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)). Raschke's trend-day rule is to hold rather than take quick partials, exiting near the close instead of overnight, while explicitly warning against averaging down as a management "fix" for a losing trade ([OptionsTradingIQ: Raschke rules](https://optionstradingiq.com/linda-raschke-rules-for-trading/)). Bellafiore's SMB rule for exits is procedural rather than emotional: "have a detailed plan for exit and stick to the plan using if/then statements" decided in advance, not renegotiated mid-trade ([TraderLion PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/)). The common "if it's not working, get out" idea shows up as a time-stop: if a setup's expected follow-through (e.g., an opening-range breakout that should immediately show trend behavior) does not materialize within a defined number of bars/minutes, the position is cut regardless of P&L, because the thesis — not just the price — has been invalidated. For news mid-trade, the practitioner norm implied across the SMB and prop material is conservative: unplanned news is treated as new information that voids the original if/then plan, triggering either an immediate size reduction/exit or a "flatten and reassess" action rather than a discretionary hold, since the original setup's statistical edge no longer applies once its causal driver (the catalyst) has changed.

**Signals a bot needs:** position entry price/time/size; live R-multiple (unrealized P&L ÷ initial risk); elapsed bars/time since entry vs. the setup's expected follow-through window; a trailing reference (structure swing low/high, moving average) per setup type; a real-time news/filing feed tagged to held symbols with a novelty/materiality flag.

**Decision rule:** At entry, register a `management_plan` per position: partial-exit R-trigger(s) (e.g., trim 33–50% at 2R), breakeven-stop trigger (move stop to entry once first partial fires or price reaches 1R), trailing-stop function (swap fixed stop for a moving trailing reference — MA or swing structure — once in profit), and a time-stop (flatten if the setup's expected confirmation, e.g., trend continuation, hasn't occurred within N bars). On each new tick/bar, evaluate all active triggers and execute the first one met — no manual override. On an incoming material news event for a held symbol, immediately downgrade the position's risk state (halve size or flatten, configurable per setup) and suppress new entries in that name until the event is reclassified, rather than letting the existing plan run unmodified against a changed catalyst.

## 6. Risk and sizing — R, daily limits, correlation, tilt control

This is the most quantified layer across sources. Minervini and Qullamaggie both anchor position risk in R (fixed % of account risked to the stop), with Minervini citing 1–2% risk per trade regardless of entry price or volatility (sizing scales inversely with stop distance) and Qullamaggie citing 0.5–1% per trade with stops never wider than 1x ADR ([FinerMarketPoints: Minervini guide](https://www.finermarketpoints.com/post/what-is-mark-minervini-s-trading-strategy-the-complete-sepa-vcp-guide); [Qullamaggie: Episodic Pivots](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/)). Prop-firm curricula formalize firm-level circuit breakers that individual discretionary traders also self-impose: a daily loss limit around 4–5% of account that halts trading for the day, and a maximum (often trailing) drawdown of roughly 8–12% that ends the trading privilege entirely — with real-time equity/exposure monitored continuously against both thresholds ([TradeZella: prop firm risk framework](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/pass-prop-firm-challenge); [ThinkCapital: drawdown rules explained](https://www.thinkcapital.com/prop-firm-drawdown-rules/); [NinjaTrader Prop: risk parameters](https://prop.ninjatrader.com/blogs/prop-firm-risk-parameters/)). Bellafiore's SMB rule to "avoid thinking in dollar terms but standardize in percentages" is the conceptual basis for R-based rather than dollar-based risk accounting ([TraderLion PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/); [SMB: 40 lessons](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/40-trading-lessons-from-the-playbook)). Raschke's related principle — running size in proportion to conviction/signal agreement, and never averaging into a loser — maps to correlation and add-on discipline ([OptionsTradingIQ: Raschke rules](https://optionstradingiq.com/linda-raschke-rules-for-trading/)). Tilt control appears repeatedly as a distinct, separately-triggered rule rather than left to trader judgment in the moment: reduce size and/or stop trading after a defined number of consecutive losers or after hitting a loss threshold, take a mandatory cooling-off period, and only re-engage at reduced size on the highest-quality setups — a pattern described across prop-trader interview material discussing post-loss "reset" protocols (secondary-source synthesis of prop-trader practice; treat the specific numeric thresholds as illustrative rather than a verified direct quote — [Forex.in.rs: Lance Breitstein insights](https://www.forex.in.rs/lance-breitstein-insights/); [SMB Training: Breitstein feature](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/wall-streets-top-trader-invites-me-to-his-penthouse-show-notes)).

**Signals a bot needs:** account equity and peak equity (for trailing drawdown); realized and open R for the day/week; consecutive-loss counter; per-symbol and per-sector open exposure; correlation matrix of currently-held names (or a proxy: shared sector/factor exposure); count of concurrent open positions.

**Decision rule:** Fix `risk_per_trade` as a % of equity (e.g., 0.5–1.5%, configurable by setup expectancy), sized as `risk_$ / (entry − stop)`. Enforce hard, non-overridable limits: (1) daily loss limit — flatten and disable new entries for the session once realized+open loss hits −X% of starting equity; (2) trailing max drawdown — same action, persists until equity recovers past the floor; (3) max concurrent positions and max aggregate exposure per sector/correlation cluster, to prevent one macro move from stacking correlated R; (4) a tilt breaker — after N consecutive losing trades (e.g., 2–3), automatically cut size by 50% and restrict entries to only the highest-historical-expectancy setup class for a cooldown period (time- or trade-count-based) before returning to normal size. All four checks run pre-trade as gates, not post-hoc advice — a trade that violates any gate is never submitted.

## 7. Reflection and adaptation — journaling, playbook stats, pruning setups

Bellafiore's stated daily discipline is to log "a trade that makes the most sense" every day into the PlayBook and archive it, building a searchable library of one's own best and worst executions annotated with the setup, the plan, and what actually happened ([TraderLion PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/); [SMB: 40 lessons](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/40-trading-lessons-from-the-playbook)). Tom Hougaard's process is similar but psychology-first: he tracks *rule adherence* as a separate metric from P&L and grades trading days on execution quality, not outcome — explicitly to avoid the trap of reinforcing a good process that happened to lose money or a bad process that happened to win ([Substack: Hougaard trading psychology lessons](https://ruchirpatel.substack.com/p/trading-psychology-lessons-from-tom); [Trasignal: Best Loser Wins summary](https://trasignal.com/blog/learn/best-loser-wins-by-tom-hougaard/)). Steenbarger's clinical framing treats meticulous record-keeping as the mechanism for surfacing recurring, otherwise-invisible behavioral patterns — the record is the diagnostic tool, not just a log ([MQL5: Psychology of Trading summary](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/759955); [Bookmap: Trading Psychology 2.0 summary](https://bookmap.com/blog/trading-psychology-2-0-summary)). The practical upshot repeated across sources is stats *segmented by setup and by regime*, not one blended win rate — because a setup's edge is regime-dependent (Section 4), pooling all trades together hides which specific setup/regime pairs are actually profitable.

**Signals a bot needs:** every trade tagged at entry with setup_id, regime classification at entry, and the specific rule/trigger fired; realized R; whether each management-plan trigger fired as designed vs. was overridden; a rule-adherence flag (did the executed trade match the pre-committed plan exactly).

**Decision rule:** Maintain a rolling expectancy table keyed by (setup_id × regime): win rate, average winning R, average losing R, expectancy = win_rate×avg_win − loss_rate×avg_loss, and sample size. Recompute on a fixed cadence (e.g., weekly, N≥20 trades minimum before acting). Auto-flag any (setup, regime) pair whose trailing expectancy turns and stays negative over a minimum sample as "suspended" — new entries for that pair are blocked until either the sample invalidates the flag or a human/higher-level process re-enables it after review. Symmetrically, increase the size multiplier for (setup, regime) pairs with the strongest trailing expectancy, operationalizing "trade your best setups more, your worst less." Track a separate `discipline_score` (executed-as-planned rate) independent of P&L, and surface it alongside expectancy so a losing streak from correct execution isn't confused with a losing streak from rule violations — mirroring Hougaard's separation of process grading from outcome grading.

## 8. Common cognitive errors and the rules that guard against them

The behavioral-finance and trading-psychology literature converge on a short list of recurring failure modes: confirmation bias and overconfidence (holding a thesis and selectively weighting confirming evidence), misattribution (crediting skill for outcomes driven by luck/regime, then over-sizing the next trade), and the FOMO → overtrading → revenge-trading cascade after a missed move or a loss ([Justmarkets summary of Steenbarger's key insights](https://justmarkets.com/trading-articles/forex/brett-steenbargers-key-insights-on-trading-psychology); search-synthesis of Steenbarger's Forbes commentary identifying misattribution and overconfidence as the most damaging biases). Lo, Repin, and Steenbarger's day-trader study specifically linked more intense emotional reactivity to gains and losses with *worse* subsequent performance, giving an empirical basis for treating emotional-intensity spikes as a risk signal, not just a subjective feeling ("Fear and Greed in Financial Markets," NBER Working Paper w11243 — [paper PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11243/w11243.pdf)). Practitioner rules against these errors are concrete and largely mechanical rather than willpower-based: if/then plans decided before entry remove in-the-moment debate ([TraderLion: PlayBook summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/)); hard daily loss limits and consecutive-loss cooldowns remove the decision of "should I stop" from the tilted trader in the moment ([TradeZella: prop risk framework](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/pass-prop-firm-challenge)); separating rule-adherence grading from P&L grading removes the incentive to rationalize a lucky rule-break as skill ([Substack: Hougaard](https://ruchirpatel.substack.com/p/trading-psychology-lessons-from-tom)); and restricting the tradeable universe to a small, pre-defined setup list (Section 3) removes the anchoring/pattern-hallucination risk of seeing a tradeable edge in arbitrary price noise ([Better System Trader: Adam Grimes](https://bettersystemtrader.com/055-adam-grimes-market-behavior/)).

**Signals a bot needs:** trades-per-hour vs. the trader's/bot's historical baseline; time-since-last-loss and consecutive-loss count; entries that occur without a corresponding registered trigger event ("off-playbook" entries); size trend after losses (are position sizes increasing after a loss — a revenge-trading tell); frequency of stop-widening or plan edits after entry.

**Decision rule:** Instrument every entry with a boolean `on_playbook` flag (true only if it passed the Section 3 gate mechanically) and hard-block any entry attempt where the flag would be false — this structurally prevents FOMO/anchoring entries outside the tested edge. Rate-limit entries per unit time (a max trades/hour or trades/day ceiling) to structurally cap overtrading regardless of how the bot "feels" about the tape. Block any post-entry widening of a stop (stops may only tighten or trail favorably, never loosen) to structurally prevent hope-driven loss extension. Trigger the Section 6 tilt breaker automatically off the consecutive-loss counter, not off a discretionary "am I OK to keep trading" check — removing exactly the self-assessment step that human research shows becomes unreliable under emotional load.

## Summary: the pipeline as software

| Stage | Core practitioner consensus | Bot module |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-market prep | Build the plan before the bell; catalyst-gate every candidate | `daily_plan` builder: macro regime tag, ranked watchlist, pre-computed triggers/invalidations |
| 2. Stock selection | Catalyst + relative volume + range expansion + RS, ranked, capped list | `in_play_score` ranker with hard catalyst gate and watchlist cap |
| 3. Setup recognition | Small registry of setups; trade the trigger, not the prediction | `playbook` registry: precondition/trigger/invalidation functions per setup |
| 4. Regime/context | Trend vs. range vs. transition changes which setups are even active | `regime_classifier` gating setup activation and size multiplier |
| 5. Trade management | Partials, breakeven stops, trailing, time stops, pre-committed plans | `management_plan` state machine evaluated every tick, no manual override |
| 6. Risk/sizing | Fixed R risk, daily/drawdown limits, correlation caps, tilt breaker | Pre-trade risk gates; hard flatten on limit breach |
| 7. Reflection | Stats by setup × regime; prune losers, size up winners | Rolling expectancy table; auto-suspend/auto-upsize by pair |
| 8. Cognitive-error guardrails | Structural rules, not willpower | `on_playbook` hard gate, trade-rate limiter, stop-only-tightens rule |

This mirrors the naturalistic-decision-making finding that experts succeed via fast, structured pattern recognition bounded by pre-committed rules rather than real-time deliberation ([Wikipedia: Naturalistic decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalistic_decision-making)) — which is also precisely the shape a rule-based/ML hybrid trading system should take: cheap, fast pattern/context classifiers feeding a small set of pre-defined, mechanically-triggered playbooks, wrapped in risk gates that never depend on the system's own in-the-moment confidence.
