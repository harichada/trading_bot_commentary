# Survey: Systematic US-Equity Strategies That Retail-Scale and Small-Prop Traders Actually Make Money With (2026)

**Scope:** US equities/equity ETFs, intraday through multi-week holds, account sizes ~$50k–$5M.
**Grading:** **A** = verified live track record (audited fund NAV, CPA-attested broker statement, investing-championship result, or population-level academic data). **B** = credible backtest or peer-reviewed study with a disclosed methodology and sample period (mostly in-sample unless an out-of-sample window is noted). **C** = anecdotal/practitioner claim, unaudited, or a backtest with thin/unverifiable methodology. **D** = marketing/course-sales content. An A-grade *P&L* is not automatically an A-grade *strategy* — it proves someone made money, not that the rules are disclosed and replicable; this is flagged wherever it applies.
**Method:** Web search and page fetches conducted 2026-09-25 against primary sources (SSRN/NBER/journal pages, Quantpedia, AQR, author blogs) and, where primary sources were paywalled or blocked, clearly-labeled secondary summaries. No number is invented; where a source could not be independently verified this is stated rather than papered over. No fully independent, third-party-audited live retail track record was found for any single strategy in this survey — that absence is itself a finding.

## 1. Opening range breakout (ORB)

**Setup (Zarattini & Aziz 2023; Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024).** Universe: QQQ and TQQQ, later extended to ~7,000 US stocks. Define the first 5-minute candle (9:30–9:35 ET) as the opening range. If it closes up, buy at the open of the second candle; if down, short (skip dojis). Stop at the opposite extreme of the opening range; the extended paper also tests a stop at 10% of 14-day ATR. Target 10R or liquidate at end of day, whichever comes first — no overnight holds. Sizing: risk exactly **1%** of equity per trade, capped at 4x intraday leverage; TQQQ routes around that leverage cap. Commissions modeled at $0.0005/share; no slippage assumed. Papers: [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622), [SSRN 4729284](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284) (both 403 to automated fetches; numbers below corroborated across three secondary extractions — [TheRobustTrader](https://therobusttrader.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable-rules-backtest-statistics-performance-analysis/), a full-text mirror at [wealth-lab.com](https://www.wealth-lab.com/api/discussion/download/pdf/6590-ORB-Strategy-pdf), and [danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). Note on the "10% risk" phrase circulating in practitioner writeups: the primary rule is **1% equity risk per trade**; "10%" appears to conflate this with the ATR-stop variant's stop-width parameter (10% of 14-day ATR).

**Results (2016–Feb 2023, 1,795 trades).** QQQ: 675% total return vs. 169% buy-and-hold, annualized alpha 33% (p=0.0025), Sharpe 1.12–1.13, **win rate 24%**, average trade **+0.13R** (asymmetric payoff, not high hit-rate). TQQQ: 1,484–1,485% total return, alpha 47%, Sharpe 1.18–1.19, max drawdown ~28%. A looser ATR-stop variant produced a 9,350% total return but the authors flag it as unrealistic beyond tiny size (implied stop width ~$0.08 on a ~$25 share). **Grade B** — neither paper discloses a genuine train/OOS split; the full stated window is the test.

**Extension — "stocks in play."** Filtering the same 5-minute breakout to the top-20 names by relative opening-volume ("stocks in play," a news/earnings-driven attention filter) turns the *unfiltered* full-universe result (3.2% annualized, Sharpe 0.48, 41.4% hit rate) into 41.6% annualized, **Sharpe 2.81**, 48.4% hit rate, 12% max drawdown. **This filter, not the breakout pattern, is what the authors credit for the edge** ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research), [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/)). **Grade B.**

**Adversarial evidence — decay.** An independent replication ([giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)) finds the gross edge (~$0.070/share) breaks even at ~2.2¢/share of slippage — near QQQ's own spread — and that 76% of the filtered strategy's P&L in one test window came from 2022 alone. Separately, [orbsetups.com](https://orbsetups.com/orb-stats/qqq-opening-range-breakout/) tracked 1,302 **live** QQQ ORB trades over the trailing 730 days through September 2026: win rates of only 49–57% depending on configuration, several configurations net-negative. **Grade B (negative)** — the clearest evidence in this survey that a published edge concentrated in an unusually volatile sample (2020–2023) decays once the tape calms down and slippage is modeled honestly.

**Regime/capacity.** Needs volatility and news flow; capacity is high on QQQ/TQQQ (into $5M+), lower per name in the single-stock "stocks in play" version but the universe is broad. **Failure modes:** slippage (the entire edge is a few cents/share), sample concentration in 2020–2023, psychological difficulty of a sub-30% win rate. **Mitigations:** trade only elevated-ATR/relative-volume days, require a genuine catalyst, never cross the spread with a market order.

## 2. Gap-and-go / gap-fade with catalysts

**Setup.** Float typically <20–50M shares, news catalyst required, 10%+ premarket gap on 3–5x+ relative volume; entry on break of the premarket high or a pullback/reclaim in the first 30–90 minutes; stop under the consolidation low ([Warrior Trading](https://www.warriortrading.com/gap-go/), [ChartsWatcher](https://chartswatcher.com/pages/blog/a-trader-s-guide-to-the-gap-and-go-strategy)). No standardized R:R is disclosed anywhere credible. **Grade D for the strategy** — this is the most course-marketing-saturated corner of the survey.

**Only A-grade datum found.** Ross Cameron's (Warrior Trading) cumulative $18,810,638 day-trading profit (2017–2025) from a $583 start is CPA-attested by SingerLewak ([source](https://www.warriortrading.com/ross-camerons-verified-day-trading-earnings/)). This verifies an **account**, not disclosed, replicable rules; treat as A for the P&L, D for the strategy claim. Reported generic gap statistics elsewhere are inconsistent (52% win/1.7 profit factor vs. 72% win/1.21 profit factor across different unaudited blog backtests) — **Grade C**.

**Large-cap gap-fade.** SPY gap-ups of 1%+ close above the prior close ~90% of the time, but open-to-close performance is flat-to-slightly-negative, and roughly half of 1%+ gaps fill intraday — a small, noisy, high-capacity fade edge. **Grade C.**

**Regime/capacity.** Small-cap version needs meme-style retail attention and caps out in the tens-to-low-hundreds of thousands of dollars per name; large-cap fade has unlimited capacity but a thin edge. **Failure modes:** 5–10x wider spreads, LULD halts, slippage on halt resumption.

## 3. VWAP reclaim / reversion

**Setup.** *Reclaim*: a 1–5 min candle closes back above VWAP after trading below it on rising relative volume; stop at the reclaim-candle low, target prior highs, 0.5–1% risk per trade ([Bulls on Wall Street](https://www.bullsonwallstreet.com/post/what-is-the-vwap-trading-indicator-and-how-to-use-it-as-a-day-trader), [TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/vwap-trading-strategy)). *Reversion*: fade N standard deviations from VWAP in range-bound sessions. **Grade D/C.**

**Evidence.** No peer-reviewed test of VWAP-reclaim as an alpha signal exists; VWAP's academic literature treats it only as an *execution benchmark* for minimizing large-order impact, not a retail entry trigger ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume-weighted_average_price)). The most concrete data point found is a cost-sensitivity result: a Python backtest of a VWAP rule swung from profitable to **-97% return** once a 0.1% commission was added ([QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/backtest-vwap-trading-strategy-python)) — direct evidence the raw signal has little-to-no edge once realistic costs apply. A separate leveraged-2x-ETF backtest with an SPY-hourly-ADX≥20 regime filter claimed ~$1,565/week over an 86-day sample ([mphinance/quantive3](https://github.com/quantive3/vwap-reclaim)); small sample, no OOS split — **Grade C, best case.** **No B-grade evidence exists for VWAP reclaim as standalone alpha.**

## 4. Momentum ignition and "first green day" / "first red day"

**Important distinction.** "Momentum ignition" is a defined academic/regulatory market-microstructure term for HFT-driven order-flow bursts designed to trigger other algos, then unwound by the igniter ([SEC-adjacent literature via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323952593_Momentum_Ignition_Price_Manipulation_and_Pricing_Mechanisms)) — this is **not the same phenomenon** as the retail small-cap pattern below, and the academic literature does not validate the retail setup.

**Setup.** Float <10–50M, price $1–20, 4–10%+ gap on 5x+ relative volume, catalyst required, minimum ~$250k/hr dollar volume for liquidity ([trademomentum.org](https://www.trademomentum.org/blog/low-float-stock-trading-for-scalpers)). "First green day": long a name's first up-day after a multi-day downtrend; "first red day": short the first down-close after a multi-day parabolic run ([ChartFanatics](https://www.chartfanatics.com/strategies/first-red-day), [TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/strategies/first-red-day-2), [StocksToTrade](https://stockstotrade.com/first-green-day-pattern/)). **Grade D for most sources.**

**Most credible disclosure.** Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie) self-reports a ~25% win rate in 2019, ~35% in 2020, risking 0.25–1% per trade with a stop no wider than 1x ADR, scaling out over 3–5 days ([interview](https://tradingresourcehub.substack.com/p/interview-qullamaggie-chat-with-traders-part1)). No third-party audit — **Grade C** — but a disclosed sub-40% win rate is internally consistent with a real, capped-loss/fat-tail R-multiple edge rather than a marketing number. By contrast, Tim Sykes's "verified" results run through Profit.ly, a platform he owns with self-entered data; independent estimates suggest the majority of his income is course sales, not trading ([Fraud Files analysis](https://www.sequenceinc.com/fraudfiles/timothy-sykes-millionaire-trading-challenge/)). **Grade D.**

**Adjacent academic evidence.** A JAR study of 1,427 short-seller attacks (2006–2020) found ~30% of targets rose over the following 3 days, about half of those gains reversing within 3 months ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1475-679X.12595)) — loosely consistent with the exhaustion logic behind first-red-day shorts, but not a direct test.

**Regime/capacity.** Needs strong retail speculative appetite (2020–2021 is the reference regime cited everywhere). Realistic capacity is sub-$100k–$250k per name. **Failure modes:** LULD halts, illiquid exits, pump-and-dump manipulation risk, crowding as scanners converge.

## 5. Relative-strength continuation vs. SPY

**Setup (Minervini Trend Template/SEPA, IBD/CAN SLIM style).** Price above rising 150/200-day MAs, 50dma above both, ≥25–30% off 52-week low and within ~25% of 52-week high, relative-strength rank ≥70; entry on breakout from a volatility contraction; hard 7–8% stop; hold weeks to months, scaling out into strength.

**Grade A (P&L only).** Mark Minervini won the US Investing Championship with 155% (1997) and 334.8% (2021) in the $1M+ division; Schwager's audited data shows ~220% average annual compounded return 1994–2000 ([Sahm Capital](https://www.sahmcapital.com/news/content/trading-wisdom-topping-the-us-charts-with-33842-returns-how-did-this-top-trader-achieve-it-2025-12-09)). This is n=1 and the rule set is not fully disclosed/replicable — A for the account, C for the general strategy claim.

**Grade B (academic core).** Jegadeesh & Titman (1993): 3–12 month winners-minus-losers earn ~1%/month, partially reversing in years 2–3 ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_(finance))). Daniel & Moskowitz, "Momentum Crashes" (JFE 2016): momentum suffers sharp drawdowns concentrated in post-crash rebounds (e.g., -45.6% March–April 2009) ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_crash)) — a well-documented failure mode. AQR reports realized momentum trading costs of 0.15–1% per trade (5–10x below naive academic estimates). CANSLIM-style backtests report 14–20% average annual returns, while live CANSLIM-linked mutual funds underperformed the S&P by roughly 0.79%/yr live — a clear paper-vs-live gap ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/canslim/)).

**Regime/capacity.** Needs a rising 200dma and risk-on tape; the worst regime is the V-shaped rebound after a crash, not the bear market itself. High capacity — scales into institutional size on large/mid caps; small-cap breakouts degrade faster with size.

## 6. Short-term mean reversion (RSI-2, IBS, weekly reversal)

**Weekly reversal (Grade B).** Universe: 100 largest US stocks; weekly rebalance, long the worst 10 performers, short the best 10 of the prior week. Backtest 1990–2009: CAGR 16.25% net, **Sharpe 1.09, max drawdown -52.94%** — a large drawdown even restricted to liquid large caps, and the paper's contribution is that the effect survives realistic costs specifically because it avoids illiquid small caps ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)). Quantpedia separately reports several reversal-type effects faded or reversed in a 2023–2025 out-of-sample check ([Quantpedia guardrails piece](https://quantpedia.com/guardrails-make-the-researcher-what-an-ai-agent-got-right-and-wrong-replicating-nine-equity-anomalies/?a=6080)) — **decay evidence, Grade B.**

**RSI-2 (Connors) and IBS.** RSI-2: buy when a 2-period RSI closes below ~5–10 while price stays above a long-term (200-day) trend filter; exit on RSI recovery above ~65–70. Independent backtests report win rates of 70–85% (one 26-year SPY test: 82% over 181 trades) but win rates fall below 60% in 2008 and March 2020, and one variant lost ~24% in 2008 despite the trend filter ([Substack backtest](https://gurufinanceinsights.substack.com/p/i-backtested-the-classic-rsi2-mean-reversion-strategy-on-26-years-of-spy)). IBS = (close−low)/(high−low): buy <0.2, avoid/short >0.8; one study finds mean next-day return +0.35% at IBS<0.2 vs. -0.13% at IBS>0.8, with combined IBS+RSI cutting time-in-market ~45% while improving returns ([NAAIM](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/00V_Alexander_Pagonidis_The-IBS-Effect-Mean-Reversion-in-Equity-ETFs-1.pdf)). Primary Connors publications with a disclosed profit factor could not be independently retrieved this session (Connors Research pages were bot-gated) — **specific rule sets graded C**; the underlying phenomenon (Jegadeesh 1990, Lehmann 1990) is **Grade B**.

**Regime/capacity.** Needs range-bound-to-mildly-declining conditions with an intact long-term uptrend; gets run over in genuine trend breaks. Reasonable capacity on large caps ($50k–several million); degrades sharply on small/micro caps.

## 7. Overnight vs. intraday return premium

**Evidence (Grade B).** Lou, Polk & Skouras, *"A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns,"* JFE 134(1), 2019, 192–213 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19300650), [LSE working paper](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)): across 14 well-known strategies, profits accrue almost entirely in one session leg (overnight or intraday), the two legs frequently carry **opposite signs**, and the spread between them forecasts future close-to-close returns — evidence of a structural, flow-driven (institutional trading/index-rebalancing) pattern rather than noise. Separately, practitioner computation shows essentially all of SPY's cumulative return since the early 1990s sits in the close-to-open leg ([HMA Quant](https://hmaquant.substack.com/p/overnight-vs-intraday-returns-the), Grade C).

**Naive implementation fails net of fees.** A QuantConnect test of the plain buy-close/sell-open SPY rule found transaction costs consumed roughly 25% of starting capital over 20 years, erasing the edge ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/15296/overnight-anomaly/)). **Grade B, negative.**

**Filtered overlay works, short sample.** Quantpedia: hold SPY overnight only when SPY > its 20dma, VIX < its 20dma, and a sentiment index > its 20dma; 2018–2021 CAGR 15.58%, **Sharpe 2.12**, max drawdown -10.97% ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/market-sentiment-and-an-overnight-anomaly)); the authors themselves recommend it as a filter/overlay, not a standalone system. **Grade B, short/narrow sample.**

**Regime/capacity.** Unlimited capacity on SPY/QQQ-class ETFs. **Failure mode:** no stop is executable while markets are closed; a bad overnight headline or gap cannot be managed intraday — treat as a directional bias (hold longs overnight, flatten shorts), not a stand-alone daily trade.

## 8. Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD)

**Setup (Quantpedia replication of Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara & Venkatachalam).** NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ ex-financials/utilities, price ≥$5; double-sort into quintiles on SUE (standardized unexpected earnings) and EAR (earnings-announcement return); enter the long top/short bottom intersection on day 2 post-announcement; hold ~60 trading days (one quarter), rebalance quarterly. Result 1987–2004: ~15% annualized, max drawdown -11.2%, combined EAR/SUE ~12.5%/yr (EAR alone ~7.55%/yr), most of the return from the long leg ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect)). **Grade B.**

**Foundational evidence.** Bernard & Thomas (1989–90): the surprise-decile return spread was positive in 41 of 48 quarters (1974–1985), producing ~8–9% abnormal return per quarter (~35% annualized before costs); 25–30% of total drift concentrates in three-day windows around the next three earnings dates despite those windows being only ~5% of trading days ([Wikipedia PEAD synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-earnings-announcement_drift)). **Grade B.**

**Decay, actively debated.** The SUE spread reportedly fell from ~5%/yr in the 1980s–90s to ~3% or lower by the late 2010s ([review discussion](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/sowi/Working_Paper/2020-04_Fink.pdf)), yet Garfinkel, Hribar & Hsiao (2024) still find a live ~5.1% three-month risk-adjusted return (~20%+ annualized) on a refined signal, and some argue the apparent decline reflects falling earnings persistence more than the anomaly disappearing. **Grade B/C** on the decay figures (full texts not always extractable).

**Regime/capacity.** Firm-specific and largely regime-agnostic, but return magnitude is largest in small/mid caps with thin analyst coverage — exactly where liquidity and short-borrow constraints bind hardest, capping realistic capacity near $1M for a small desk. **Failure mode:** execution/slippage erosion of a single-digit-percent quarterly edge; drift reversal risk if held through the next announcement.

## 9. News / catalyst plays with sentiment

**Foundational evidence (Grade B).** Tetlock (2007), *J. Finance*, "Giving Content to Investor Sentiment": elevated media pessimism predicts short-term downward price pressure followed by reversion toward fundamentals, with the exploitable signal being a days-long reversal rather than a persistent trend ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=685145)).

**2024–2026 LLM-sentiment claims — mostly not credible.** A wave of 2025 papers claims large Sharpe/return improvements from LLM-scored headline sentiment (e.g., one arXiv paper reports an SMA-crossover Sharpe rising from 0.34 to 3.47 with sentiment overlay, [arXiv 2502.01574](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01574v1)). A critical review, "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in GPT Sentiment Predictions" ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322)), finds these results are frequently contaminated by **look-ahead bias** (models effectively trained on data spanning the evaluation window) and **label leakage** (sentiment classifiers trained on labels derived from the same period's realized returns, with no genuine train/test split). This is a systemic problem across current retail "AI reads the news and trades" content. **Grade D for essentially all 2024–2026 retail LLM-sentiment trading claims found in this survey.**

**One replicable, negative practitioner test.** A documented backtest on the 30 DJIA names, 2,072 headlines, 2018–2022, top-5%-sentiment entries with a 25% profit target and 1% commission: GPT-3.5 sentiment scoring returned 41.0%, RavenPack sentiment 41.0%, plain buy-and-hold **58.1%** ([Dubach](https://philippdubach.com/posts/trading-on-market-sentiment/)). **Grade C, but the one apples-to-apples test found — and it lost to the benchmark.**

**Capacity.** Professional feeds (Benzinga: 600–900 headlines/day, ~25ms latency) reach colocated institutional algos in single-digit milliseconds; retail bots get the structurally slower residual signal. **Failure modes:** fake/retracted headlines, noisy or manipulated sentiment, crowding, and latency disadvantage vs. HFT.

## 10. Pairs trading / statistical arbitrage

**Setup (Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst).** Normalize prices to $1 cumulative indices; 12-month formation picks the minimum-sum-of-squared-deviation pairs; 6-month trading window; open at a 2-standard-deviation divergence, close on convergence; trade the top 20 pairs. 1962–2002: annualized excess return up to 11–12% net of estimated costs, **Sharpe 1.22**, volatility 5.85%, max drawdown ~17% ([NBER w7032](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7032)). **Grade B.**

**Well-documented decay.** Raw profitability declined markedly post-1988 even as risk-adjusted returns held up somewhat; Do & Faff (2010) find top-20-pair mean monthly excess return falling from 0.86% (1962–1988) to 0.37% (1989–2002) to 0.24% (2003–2009) ([summary](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/41067/)); broader stat-arb literature describes returns "reduced considerably" from 1998–2007 due to competition, culminating in the August 2007 "quant quake" correlated unwind ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_arbitrage)). Ernie Chan's published GLD/GDX pair stayed profitable roughly two years post-publication, then broke down; adding a USO third leg (0.535·GLD − 0.739·GDX + 0.029·USO) restored profitability 2006–2010 ([epchan blog](http://epchan.blogspot.com/2011/06/when-cointegration-of-pair-breaks-down.html)). A 2012–2015 QuantRocket replication on 110 cointegrated ETF pairs found top-pair Sharpes of 0.69–1.03, "performing well the first two years out of sample, then deteriorating" ([QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/pairs-trading-still-viable/)).

**Regime/capacity.** Needs genuine, stable economic linkage between legs; breaks down in regime shifts (M&A, sector rotation, systemic dislocations — though the strategy performed strongly during the 2008–09 dislocation itself). Sub-$5M is arguably an advantage versus institutional crowding, but expect sub-0.5%/month excess and a requirement for continuous pair rediscovery, not a static, "set and forget" pair.

## 11. Trend following on daily bars

**Evidence.** Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), "Time Series Momentum": across 58 liquid futures/forwards, a security's own trailing 12-month return positively predicts its next-month return, positive for every instrument tested, persisting roughly a year before partially reversing ([AQR summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum)). Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (2017): positive in every decade since 1880, profitable in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40-portfolio drawdowns ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). **Grade B.** Live: AQR Managed Futures (AQMRX) returned -0.30% (2020), -0.94% (2021), **+35.41% (2022)**, +2.02% (2023) — the closest thing to A-grade, verified-live evidence in this survey. Trend following is famously regime-dependent: the original 1980s Turtle program reportedly earned an aggregate ~$175M over five years, but the same rules produced "a drastic drop in performance after 1986, and flat performance from 1996–2009" backtested forward — decay directly documented, not just alleged ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)). A broad CTA index (CASAM CISDM) compounded 14.52%/yr 1980–2010 vs. 7.04% for US stocks — but 89% of the $11.51B in gains across 63 managed-futures funds 2003–2012 went to fees and expenses, a large gross-vs-net gap ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managed_futures_account)).

**Retail-accessible variant.** Dual Momentum GEM (Antonacci): monthly, hold cash-equivalent bonds if US equities trail T-bills trailing 12 months; else hold whichever of US/ex-US equities has the higher 12-month return. 57-year backtest: CAGR 14.5%, max drawdown -21.6%, Sharpe 0.79, ~1.5 trades/year. **Grade B**, with documented sensitivity to the exact lookback/rebalance date.

**Regime/capacity.** Works in sustained directional moves; the single-name-equity version has materially weaker support than the diversified-futures version (individual stocks are noted to be more mean-reverting). Very high capacity at the futures/ETF level — this is what billion-dollar CTAs run; single-stock trend baskets are noisier and lower-capacity.

## Context: the base rate every strategy above must beat

Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (Taiwan Stock Exchange day traders, 1992–2006): fewer than 1% of day traders reliably earned positive abnormal returns net of fees ([Haas working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)). Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (Brazilian equity-futures day traders): of traders persisting 300+ days, **97% lost money**, only 1.1% earned more than minimum wage, and 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's salary ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_trading)). SEC v. Matlock (2022) charged eight "successful trader" social-media influencers in a $100M pump-and-dump built on their own followers' flow ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-221)). **Grade A/B context evidence** — large-sample, exchange-record-based. Anything marketed primarily through a course or subscription should be presumed Grade D until an independent test exists.

## What the credible (A/B) strategies have in common

1. **Selectivity beats frequency, and a filter usually *is* the edge.** The identical ORB pattern went from Sharpe 0.48 (unfiltered) to Sharpe 2.81 filtered to "stocks in play." PEAD filters on surprise magnitude, momentum filters on relative strength, pairs trading filters on cointegration quality — every B-grade family is a filter over a much larger, unprofitable universe.
2. **An explicit regime or trend gate.** RSI-2/relative-strength systems use a 200dma filter; the overnight overlay uses VIX/sentiment filters; GEM uses an absolute-momentum cash switch. Strategies without one (naive daily overnight trading, unfiltered ORB) die on costs or decay fastest.
3. **Fixed-fractional, volatility-scaled risk per trade.** ORB risks exactly 1% via a formula; Turtle/trend systems size by ATR ("N"); Qullamaggie risks 0.25–1%; Minervini caps losses at 7–8%. Disclosed win rates range from 24% (ORB) to 82% (RSI-2) — what they share is a loss bounded and known before the trade, not a high hit rate.
4. **Costs are modeled, not assumed away — and usually kill the paper edge.** The VWAP backtest that flipped from profitable to -97% on a 0.1% commission, the ORB replication's 2.2¢/share breakeven, the naive overnight strategy's 25%-of-capital fee drag, and CANSLIM funds underperforming by ~0.79%/yr live vs. +14–20% backtest are the same lesson repeated across five unrelated families.
5. **Every credible edge visibly decays**, and none has an independently audited, live retail track record: ORB weakened from the 2016–23 sample to near-coin-flip live results through 2026; PEAD's spread has shrunk for four decades; pairs-trading returns "decreased considerably" post-1998; Turtle-style trend following went flat for 13 years (1996–2009). Treat every number above as an upper bound and pair live deployment with decay monitoring.

## Implications for a bot

- **Build the selectivity/regime filter before the entry rule.** A relative-volume or "stocks-in-play" gate, a 200dma trend filter, or a VIX/sentiment gate is the single most repeated ingredient across every B-grade result here.
- **Model 2¢+/share slippage on anything intraday before trusting a backtest**, and model at least a few basis points of round-trip cost on anything with daily-or-faster turnover — several of the best-documented "edges" in this survey are within a hair of that cost line.
- **Prefer the long side and lower trade frequency.** PEAD, relative strength, GEM and the overnight bias earn most of their return long, with 1.5–4 round trips a year for the swing/position families; the daily-frequency naive strategy in this survey (plain overnight) is the one destroyed by fees.
- **Skip small-cap gap-and-go, VWAP reclaim, momentum ignition/first-green-day, and 2024-26-style LLM headline sentiment as standalone alpha sources.** None has B-grade evidence, all are capacity-capped or latency-disadvantaged, and the loudest current claims (LLM sentiment) are demonstrably contaminated by look-ahead bias. Their only defensible use is as a secondary filter (e.g., "stocks in play" attention) layered onto a strategy that already has a real edge.
