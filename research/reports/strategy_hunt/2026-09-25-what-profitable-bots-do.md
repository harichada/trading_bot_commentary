# What Profitable Algo Trading Bots Actually Do (US Equities, as of Sept 2026)

**Scope:** survey of open-source frameworks, GitHub repos, LLM-agent trading research, and practitioner postmortems, to inform design choices for a ~$45k retail Schwab account doing intraday + swing trading. All claims below are sourced; URLs are inline. Evidence strength is flagged per claim because most of what circulates about "profitable bots" is marketing, backtest, or paper trading — not verified live results.

**Headline finding:** across dozens of frameworks, repos, and papers searched, there is almost no publicly available, independently verifiable evidence of a small retail bot sustaining live US-equities profitability net of costs. What *is* well evidenced, repeatedly and from multiple independent angles, is *why bots fail* — overfitting, ignored transaction costs, no regime awareness, and missing kill switches. The design implication is to treat this report less as "copy what works" and more as "avoid the well-documented ways to lose."

## 2. GitHub repos claiming live profitability, and what actually held up

Across a broad GitHub/Reddit/HN/forum search, **no repo was found with strong, independently verifiable evidence of sustained live US-equities profitability** (broker statement, audited equity curve, or third-party-verified track record tied to a real account). That absence is itself the finding. What did surface:

- **Dkingsley1/Schwab-Trading-Bots** — Schwab-integrated research platform that self-gates live trading behind a "12/14 required sections" checklist and ships with `ALLOW_ORDER_EXECUTION=0` by default; author explicitly disclaims that health/safety grades are not profitability proof. Notable as a rare example of a project refusing to over-claim. ([repo](https://github.com/Dkingsley1/Schwab-Trading-Bots))
- **IlJuniorlI/TradingBot** — Schwab equities + 0DTE ETF options bot, 14 strategies, has real risk controls (max notional/trade, daily loss limits, adaptive trailing stops) but zero published backtest or live numbers. ([repo](https://github.com/IlJuniorlI/TradingBot))
- **ryanappel42/Algorithmic-Trading-Bot** — XGBoost on 20 large-caps, self-reports +8.65% growth / 67% signal accuracy, but README states plainly "all trading uses paper money — no real funds are at risk." Good example of impressive metrics that aren't live evidence. ([repo](https://github.com/ryanappel42/Algorithmic-Trading-Bot))
- **davidalv2/algo-trading-bot** — Momentum/trend/stat-arb via Lumibot+Alpaca; ~70% annualized reported, but this is a 2024 backtest on 5 mega-caps, live capability limited to Alpaca paper. ([repo](https://github.com/davidalv2/algo-trading-bot))
- **jononon/algo-trading-schwab** — Risk-on/risk-off rotation into 3x leveraged ETFs (SOXL/TQQQ/UPRO/TECL) on RSI + bond relative strength; no performance data published; the leveraged-ETF concentration is itself a risk-management red flag. ([repo](https://github.com/jononon/algo-trading-schwab))
- **steveman1123/stonkBot** — Penny-stock bot with a genuine live toggle; self-reports 1.06–1.17%/day average over Jul 2020–Feb 2021. Closest thing found to a dated, first-person live claim, but unaudited and self-described as the author's first project. ([repo](https://github.com/steveman1123/stonkBot))
- **HN "agent-run trading fund with real capital"** — 131 strategies tested, reports +57.9%, but author is explicit this came from "a single concentrated session... 3–8 AM ET" on a ~$247 account and says "annualizing a three-hour return would be ridiculous." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47381593))
- **HN "AI Quant Agent trading live with my own $1000"** — LLM investment-committee over deterministic signals, human-in-the-loop approval; one week of live data at post time; author invites the community to predict how fast they'll lose the money. ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47347022))
- **HN "12 AI bots predicting stocks, every call public"** — Not live trading, but a transparent public leaderboard (Claude, ChatGPT, others) with Bayesian-smoothed confidence intervals; after two months, **no bot statistically outperformed the market** — a rare, honestly reported null result. ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49014412))
- **EliteTrader forum "HFT Bots — Live Results Comparison"** — Poster claims institutional-scale results, never posts data; forum reacts with default skepticism. Illustrates the community's baseline distrust of unverified claims. ([thread](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/automated-high-frequency-trading-bots-gpu-cpu-live-results-comparison-institutional-scale.377717/))
- **Quantopian's shutdown (2020)** — The most consequential failure case for crowdsourced retail-equities quant. Quantopian's own research ("All That Glitters Is Not Gold") found user backtests had little OOS predictive value; the hosted fund failed to generate required live returns and the platform closed, returning capital. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-12-16/quant-trading-platform-quantopian-closes-down), [retrospective](https://quantrocket.medium.com/3-takeaways-from-quantopian-shutting-down-15823c8e4247))
- **Knight Capital, Aug 1 2012** — The canonical algo-failure case: a dormant flag-triggered code path from an incomplete 8-server deployment caused runaway order generation with no execution-confirmation checks, producing $7.65B in unintended positions and a $440M loss in 45 minutes. Root causes were no automated kill switch and no incident-response runbook. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2012/08/02/157824026/knight-capital-says-it-lost-440-million-because-of-computer-glitch), [postmortem](https://specbranch.com/posts/knight-capital/))

**Cross-cutting theme:** the repos that are honest about evidence quality (Dkingsley1, the HN null-result leaderboard) are more useful to learn from than the ones with flashy numbers, because the flashy numbers reliably turn out to be paper trading, tiny samples, or cherry-picked windows.

## 3. LLM-agent trading bots: claims vs. what evaluation shows

| System | Claim | What holds up under scrutiny | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TradingAgents** (multi-agent debate framework) | Beats Buy&Hold/MACD/KDJ&RSI baselines by 6–25pp on AAPL/GOOGL/AMZN. ([arXiv 2412.20138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138)) | Evaluation window is only Jan–Mar 2024, 3 tickers. Open GitHub issue #805 argues the underlying LLM's training weights already encode 2023-24 events, invalidating the backtest's causal validity; maintainers haven't responded. ([issue](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/issues/805)) TradingAgents is directly named as leakage-affected in the "Profit Mirage" study below. | C/D |
| **FinRL / FinRL-X** | 6-month Alpaca **paper-trading** test (Oct 2025–Mar 2026), ensemble strategy, +19.76% vs SPY/QQQ. ([arXiv 2603.21330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21330)) | Real broker-integrated paper trading (better than pure backtest), but authors themselves say the result is "not intended to establish statistically significant alpha." DRL agents are documented to be prone to hyperparameter-driven overfitting to one regime, which is why AI4Finance built FinRL-Meta with more diverse environments. ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3490354.3494366)) | B- (real paper-trading pipeline, statistically weak) |
| **FinMem / FinAgent** (memory + reflection) | FinAgent claims >36% average profit improvement over 9 baselines across 6 datasets. ([arXiv 2402.18485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18485)) | An independent replication check found FinMem's reported 23.26% cumulative return on MSFT **flipped to -22.04%** under a slightly different, equally defensible window once transaction costs were included — a sign reversal consistent with overfitting/cherry-picked windows. ([Klement Substack](https://klementoninvesting.substack.com/p/the-performance-decay-of-llm-trading), secondary source, unverified against primary study) | D |
| **Profit Mirage** (arXiv 2510.07920) | Systematically tests FinGPT, Fin-LLaMA, FinMem, FinReport, QuantAgent, FinCon, **TradingAgents**, FinRobot for information leakage. | Finds leakage inflates backtest performance across the board; performance drops significantly under leakage-controlled evaluation. The single most important critical paper found in this survey. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07920)) | A- (rigorous critical audit, preprint) |
| **SoK: Trading Agents or Market Crashers?** (arXiv 2609.19705) | Audits 15 academic financial LLM trading schemes for robustness/security. | 80% fail at least one core robustness metric under simulated flash-crash conditions; 100% show exploitable security vulnerabilities (compromised data sources, prompt/agent attacks). | A- (structured audit, preprint) |
| **StockBench** (arXiv 2510.02209) | Contamination-free benchmark, 20 DJIA stocks, Mar–Jul 2025 (post-training-cutoff to avoid leakage). | Most LLM agents **struggle to beat simple buy-and-hold**; a few show promise only on risk-adjusted metrics. | B+ (methodologically careful) |
| Replication cited via Klement Substack | 5 SOTA LLM methods (GPT-4o, training cutoff Oct 2023) tested in-window (Q2-Q3 2021: 30-44% returns) vs post-cutoff (Q3-Q4 2024: 9-22% returns) despite similar (~13.5%) index returns both periods. | Suggests models partly "memorize" rather than predict; primary paper could not be independently located/verified. | C (secondary source) |

**Synthesis on what actually adds value:** the most credible, methodologically careful papers (Profit Mirage, SoK, StockBench) are *skeptical* of LLM-agent outperformance, not supportive. Multi-agent debate and "beats baseline by double digits" headline numbers rest on short windows and are named specifically as leakage-affected. Memory/reflection has some generic support in the broader LLM-agent literature but the one finance-specific independent check (FinMem MSFT sign-flip) contradicts it. Risk-management sub-agents are unproven — no ablation study isolating their contribution was found, and the SoK audit suggests risk-handling is actually where these systems fail most under stress. The strongest, most replicable message across this literature is that **simple technical/buy-and-hold baselines are hard to beat once leakage and costs are controlled.**

## 4. Common architecture: what the ones that work share, and why the ones that fail fail

**Signal selectivity.** Practitioner consensus (Robot Wealth, Billion Dollar Algorithms) is that durable retail edges look "unattractive, noisy, small" — which is why institutions ignore them and they persist — and that sub-5-minute timeframes are largely noise best left to HFT. Costs scale with trade frequency: a 5%-return strategy with 3% costs isn't viable, so selectivity is partly a cost-avoidance mechanism. ([Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/), [Huntersalgo](https://huntersalgo.com/guides/backtesting-vs-live-trading-results))

**Regime filters.** Widely repeated practitioner pattern: classify volatility regime first (crash/high-vol overrides everything), then trend-vs-chop, and gate or resize strategies accordingly ("when a system trades is often more important than how it trades"). No rigorous, peer-reviewed quantification of regime-filter efficacy for retail equities was found in this search — treat this as a strong practitioner consensus, not an established empirical result. ([PyQuantLab](https://pyquantlab.medium.com/regime-filtered-trend-strategy-a-market-adaptive-trend-following-system-fa933e001237), [QuantMonitor](https://quantmonitor.net/how-to-identify-market-regimes-and-filter-strategies-by-trend-and-volatility/))

**Risk management.** Full Kelly carries roughly a 1-in-3 chance of a 50% drawdown, hence near-universal use of fractional (often half-) Kelly, or volatility-targeted sizing for practitioners uncertain of true edge. ([QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/articles/foundations-position-sizing)) Quantopian's own large-N crowdsourced dataset found most user-submitted alphas were overfitting/cost-ignoring illusions. ([Robot Wealth retrospective](https://robotwealth.com/my-thoughts-on-quantopians-closing/)) Kill-switch patterns repeatedly cited: a daily-loss-limit halt and a max-drawdown-from-peak-equity halt (the latter catches slow bleeds the former misses), with the common pitfall being reliance on internal bookkeeping instead of reconciling against actual broker state. ([kill-switch doc](https://skills.himanshujangir.com/skills/kill-switch-and-drawdown-circuit-breakers/))

**Execution quality.** Slippage is layered (spread, market impact, signal-to-fill timing lag) and often gets *worse* as account size grows even for small accounts, because order size grows with equity; a commonly cited guardrail is keeping position size under ~5% of a symbol's minimum daily dollar turnover. ([Huntersalgo](https://huntersalgo.com/guides/backtesting-vs-live-trading-results)) Backtests silently assume frictionless historical fills, which is cited as the single largest driver of backtest/live divergence. ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/backtesting-limitations-slippage-and-liquidity-explained/)) Separately, the SEC approved FINRA's elimination of the $25,000 PDT minimum (effective June 4 2026, broker rollout through Oct 2027) — largely moot for a $45k account, which was already above the old threshold. ([Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum))

**Monitoring/ops.** "If a system can trade automatically, it must also be able to stop itself automatically" is the repeated framing. Cited failure modes in kill-switch implementations: resting orders meant to force-flatten don't fill promptly during a breach, alerts are left unguarded in the halt path, and internal position state isn't reconciled against the broker's actual state. One concrete developer anecdote: a bot profitable in backtest blew up in paper trading, fixed by borrowing a circuit-breaker pattern from distributed-systems engineering. ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/qcautomation/how-i-added-a-circuit-breaker-to-my-ai-trading-bot-and-why-every-algo-needs-one-4p95))

**Ranked reasons systems fail** (by frequency of citation across sources):
1. **Overfitting/curve-fitting** — the dominant theme across nearly every source. ([Sigmentic](https://www.sigmentic.com/blog/detect-avoid-overfitting-trading-strategies), [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/what-is-overfitting-in-algorithmic-trading))
2. **Ignoring transaction costs** — the single best-quantified finding in this whole survey: Alpha Architect's study of 215 bank-promoted "alternative beta" strategies found median backtest Sharpe 1.20 fell to median live Sharpe 0.31 (a 73% median deterioration); only 8.4% had live Sharpe ≥ backtest; 30% went live-negative; complexity correlated with worse deterioration. ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/05/beware-of-backtest-overfitting/)) — note: institutional sample, not retail-specific, but the most rigorous empirical number in this entire report.
3. **Survivorship bias in backtests.**
4. **No regime awareness / edge decay** — "if you can't adapt... you'll always be wiped out eventually as regimes shift."
5. **Correlation blowup among nominally independent signals** — strategies with low return-correlation can still share 0.6+ drawdown-correlation; recommended fix is measuring drawdown correlation directly, not equity-curve correlation. ([algomatictrading](https://algomatictrading.substack.com/p/how-correlation-between-strategies))
6. **No kill switch / inadequate risk controls** — best evidenced by Knight Capital at institutional scale, one concrete retail anecdote found.
7. **Psychological override by the human operator** — closing winners early from fear, over-optimizing win-rate (which drives curve-fitting), impatience driving unrealistic expectations. (Single blog source, anecdotal.)
8–9. **Excess leverage and data snooping** — mentioned across sources but not treated as standalone, well-evidenced themes in what was found; flag as weakly evidenced, not as unimportant.

## 5. Concrete, implementable ideas for our bot, graded by evidence

| # | Idea | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build in a hard daily-loss-limit kill switch **and** a separate max-drawdown-from-peak-equity halt, with reconciliation against actual Schwab account state (not just internal bookkeeping) before resuming. | Repeated practitioner pattern; failure mode of not reconciling is specifically cited; Knight Capital is the extreme institutional cautionary tale. ([kill-switch doc](https://skills.himanshujangir.com/skills/kill-switch-and-drawdown-circuit-breakers/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2012/08/02/157824026/knight-capital-says-it-lost-440-million-because-of-computer-glitch)) | C (practitioner consensus + one severe institutional case study) |
| 2 | Model transaction costs (spread + impact + timing slippage) explicitly and conservatively in every backtest before trusting any Sharpe number, and re-check live Sharpe against backtest Sharpe regularly — expect material decay. | Alpha Architect's 215-strategy study is the most rigorous quantified evidence in this survey: median 73% Sharpe deterioration backtest-to-live. ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/05/beware-of-backtest-overfitting/)) | B (real empirical study, though institutional sample) |
| 3 | Keep any single position under a small fraction (~5% cited) of the symbol's average daily dollar turnover, and size positions with fractional-Kelly or volatility-targeting rather than fixed dollar amounts, given the account is only ~$45k. | Standard quant practice, consistent across multiple practitioner sources; math on Kelly fragility is well-established. ([QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/articles/foundations-position-sizing), [Huntersalgo](https://huntersalgo.com/guides/backtesting-vs-live-trading-results)) | B/C (established theory + repeated practitioner application) |
| 4 | Be skeptical of any LLM-agent component (news reasoning, multi-agent debate, "trading committee") delivering alpha on its own; if used, treat it as a qualitative overlay/veto on top of a technical/quant core, not the primary signal generator, and evaluate it only on strictly leakage-safe, post-cutoff, cost-inclusive data. | Profit Mirage and SoK are rigorous critical audits showing leakage inflates nearly every LLM-trading-agent result; StockBench shows most LLM agents don't beat buy-and-hold once contamination is controlled. ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07920), [SoK](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19705), [StockBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02209)) | A- (multiple independent, methodologically careful critical papers) |
| 5 | Prefer strategies that are simple enough to "reconcile perfectly with backtests" and trade semi-regularly (not ultra-high-frequency), rather than complex multi-parameter systems — complexity correlated with worse live deterioration in the Alpha Architect study, and QuantConnect's own adoption data favors simpler, more transparent strategies. | Two independent sources converge on the same conclusion from different angles (a vendor's internal adoption analysis, and an academic-style backtest/live deterioration study). ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/12017/success-factors-in-the-alpha-market-an-analysis), [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/05/beware-of-backtest-overfitting/)) | B/C |
| 6 | Add an explicit volatility/regime gate (e.g., VIX level or realized-vol bucket) that can shut off or downsize strategies rather than assuming one model works across all regimes — this is exactly what QuantConnect says its Alpha Streams submitters got wrong. | Practitioner consensus plus a direct vendor admission of the opposite failure mode. ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/13441/alpha-streams-refactoring-2-0/), [PyQuantLab](https://pyquantlab.medium.com/regime-filtered-trend-strategy-a-market-adaptive-trend-following-system-fa933e001237)) | C (no rigorous quantification found, but converging qualitative evidence) |
| 7 | If running multiple "independent" strategies concurrently, measure and monitor **drawdown correlation** between them, not just return correlation — they can look diversified on paper and still blow up together. | Named as a common, specific portfolio-construction mistake with a concrete fix. ([algomatictrading](https://algomatictrading.substack.com/p/how-correlation-between-strategies)) | C (single but specific and mechanistically sound source) |
| 8 | Avoid concentrating rotation strategies into leveraged ETFs (3x products) as a way to express directional views, since this compounds regime-timing risk on top of strategy risk without adding real diversification. | Observed as a design choice with no accompanying risk controls in the jononon/algo-trading-schwab repo — a negative example, not a validated recommendation. ([repo](https://github.com/jononon/algo-trading-schwab)) | D (anecdotal negative example) |
| 9 | Publish/self-track results the way the most credible sources in this survey did — with explicit small-sample caveats, R-multiples or risk-adjusted units rather than raw %, and honest null-result reporting — as an internal discipline to catch self-deception before scaling capital. | The HN 12-bot leaderboard and RB Trading Substack are the two examples found that reported null or caveated results honestly, in contrast to the many repos that overstate paper-trading results as "live." ([HN leaderboard](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49014412), [RB Trading](https://rbtrading.substack.com/p/real-results-with-real-capital)) | C (practice pattern, not a performance claim) |

## Conclusion

The clearest signal from this survey is negative-space: there is a near-total absence of credible, independently verified live-profitability evidence for small retail US-equities bots, across open-source ecosystems, GitHub, and the LLM-agent research literature. The platforms that once tried to certify live performance (QuantConnect Alpha Streams v1, Quantopian) either shut the certification down or produced internal research showing most submissions were overfit. The most rigorous quantitative finding available — Alpha Architect's 215-strategy backtest-to-live Sharpe deterioration study — and the most rigorous LLM-agent critiques (Profit Mirage, SoK, StockBench) both point the same direction: apparent edges shrink or disappear once leakage, costs, and out-of-sample discipline are enforced. For a $45k Schwab account, the actionable posture is conservative by construction — simple, cost-aware, regime-gated, hard-limited by kill switches, skeptical of LLM components as primary alpha sources, and honest in its own internal performance reporting.
